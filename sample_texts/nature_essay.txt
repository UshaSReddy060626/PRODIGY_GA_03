The forest wakes slowly in the early morning light. Mist curls between the tall pines like smoke from a quiet fire. Birds call to one another across the valley, their voices bright against the cool air. A stream moves over smooth stones, carrying small leaves toward the river below.

Deer move carefully through the underbrush, pausing often to listen. The wind carries the scent of wet earth and pine sap through the clearing. Sunlight breaks through the canopy in thin golden shafts, warming patches of moss on the forest floor. Somewhere a woodpecker taps steadily against an old oak tree.

By midday the forest is alive with sound. Insects hum among the wildflowers growing near the edge of the meadow. A hawk circles high above, searching the open field for movement. The stream widens here, slowing into a calm pool where small fish dart beneath the surface.

As evening approaches, the light turns soft and orange. Shadows stretch long across the forest floor, and the air grows cooler. The birds grow quiet one by one, and the forest settles into a gentle stillness. Stars begin to appear above the treetops as the last light fades from the sky.

The mountain stands silent through all of this, patient and unmoving. Clouds drift slowly past its peak, changing shape as the wind carries them onward. Snow lingers near the summit even in the warmer months, feeding the streams that wind down through the forest below. Travelers who climb high enough can see the entire valley spread out beneath them, rivers glinting like silver threads through the green.

Winter changes everything. Snow falls quietly over the sleeping forest, softening every branch and stone. The stream slows to a trickle beneath a thin sheet of ice. Animal tracks cross the white ground in careful, deliberate lines. The world feels smaller, quieter, and somehow more honest beneath the snow.
